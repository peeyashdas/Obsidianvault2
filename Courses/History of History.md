**Leopold non Ranke (1795 - 1886)**
- Born in Thuringia, philological training in Leipzig - trained in Greek and Latin
-  Father was descendant of Lutheran pastor
- 1824: History of Latin and Teutonic Nations , 1494 - 1535
- Professor at the University of Berlin 
- Hegel as colleague (1770 - 1835)
	- Dialectical synthesis - cycle of conflicts and contradictions within an overarching rational development embodied in a secular state
	- At the same time, view of history as the progressive revelation of God’s reason or spirit world
- Ranke declared that he did not follow the Hegel’s notion of past, he believed in the past as it was and not an overarching scheme of things. However, he believed (as Hegel ) that states and societies appeared were divinely ordained and things happened as they were providentially schemed. 
- He was the part of the forces that were reacting to the French Revolution and took a conservative stance of the past. The past is to be regarded as no less valid as the new ideas of revolution coming our way. (Close to the government and received funding from the Prussian governments to conduct a four year field work.)
- Achievements - 
	1. Historical seminar (1825 - 1831)
		- Replicated model 
		- Professionalisation of the historian 
		- Training in history was becoming a serious affair and accessible to a number of students - produced scholars who were prepared for hard-work - 
		- Highly gendered environment - dominated by men - male milieu was sustained by the labour of the female secretary and administrative support of wives and partners
	2. Archival Fieldwork
		- State documents
		- Archival work as coming of age ritual 
	3. Emphasis on details
		- Search for “facts”
	4. (Claimed) abstention from moral judgement
		- “wie es eigentlich gewesen
