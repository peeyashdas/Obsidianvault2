
But the knowledge of the past is something progressive which is constantly transforming and perfecting itself.           - Marc Bloch, The Historian’s Craft

Writing in the period marked by war, imperialism and fascism, Marc Bloch’s observation highlights the evolving nature of knowledge production in historical research. As a pioneer of the Annales School, Bloch, alongside Lucien Febvre and later Fernand Braudel, contributed to a critical shift in historical writing, and are indispensable figures in the mapping of history writing  However, the historiographical enterprise emerged in 19th century , much before the conception of the Annales school, undertaken by Leopold Von Ranke. Pioneer of the historical seminar, Ranke led the way to primary archival documents and objective reconstruction of the past (_wie es eigentlich gewesen_ — "as it actually happened"). However, he was the part of the forces that were reacting to the French Revolution and took a conservative stance of the past. He believed that the past was to be regarded as no less valid as the new ideas of revolution coming their way. His approach towards history was based on empirical evidence, and emphasis on fact. 

He departed from Hegelian dialectics and declared that he did not follow Hegel’s speculative philosophy of history. He believed in the past as it was and not an overarching scheme of things (weltgeist). However, he believed (as Hegel ) that states and societies appeared were divinely ordained and things happened as they were providentially schemed. However, Ranke’s academic vision was limited by his affiliations with state power, and most of his historical works were political histories of meta institutions and imperial powers. 

In the mid-nineteenth century, Karl Marx challenged elite-centred interpretations of history through his theory of historical materialism, developed partly in response to the inequalities of industrial capitalism in England. Rejecting Hegel’s idealism while retaining his dialectical method, Marx grounded historical change in material conditions and modes of production, comprising forces ( raw materials, technology, labor, knowledge) and relations of production. Contradictions within these modes generated class antagonisms, particularly between the bourgeoisie and proletariat under capitalism, where exploitation through surplus labour and false consciousness sustained class divisions. Marx envisaged class struggle ultimately leading to communism. His ideas influenced historical writing by foregrounding economic structures, class relations and ordinary people.

In 1929, Marc Bloch and Lucien Febvre founded the journal Annales d’histoire économique et sociale, marking a significant departure from conventional political history. While Marxism challenged elite-centred historical narratives by foregrounding material conditions and class relations, the Annales School broadened historical inquiry through its emphasis on environment, climate and demography (Lynn Hunt). Fernand Braudel further developed this approach through the concept of the longue durée, emphasising the persistence of geographical, economic and social structures over extended periods. The Annales School thus challenged event-centred history by presenting the past as a multilayered process operating across different timescales.

British Marxist historians, meanwhile, sought to recover the experiences and agency of ordinary people within a Marxist framework. E. P. Thompson's _The Making of the English Working Class_ (1963), for instance, rejected the treatment of workers as passive products of industrialisation. Instead, Thompson argued that the working class was 'present at its own making', emphasising the agency and lived experiences through which class consciousness developed. In his study of industrial capitalism, Thompson also demonstrated that economic transformations could not be separated from changes in culture and everyday life. However, feminist historians like Joan W Scott subsequently criticised Thompson for overlooking how working-class language and culture could marginalise women, despite their participation in working-class movements. Such criticism exposed the limitations of existing approaches to social history and raised questions about whose experiences were represented in historical narratives.

Cultural theories brought a tectonic shift in the “paradigms of history”, including Marxism, modernization , the Annales school and identity politics. According to Lynn Hunt cultural theories traces their origins primarily to Britain (cultural studies), France (postmodernism), and the United States (cultural history).  Cultural theories that emerged in the growing criticism of the four paradigms in the 1960s, 70s and 80s, insisted that culture has its own autonomous logic and existence. 

Soon historians trained in the four historiographical paradigms were revising and including cultural theories in their historical writing. Joan Scott was a leading proponent of post - modernism among historians. The Annales- school historians Roger Chartier and Jacques Revel  pioneered the cultural turn in France. However, the crossover from identity politics was less apparent. For example, the path to queer studies was led by anthropologist Gayle Rubin and philosopher Judith Butler. 

In the paradigm of identity politics,  the treatment of race, gender and indigeneity as a homogenous category came under fire. Lynn Hunt writes that scholars moved by cultural theories pushed for history of gender and not just women. Similarly, the study of African Americans, Asians Americans and Latinos was challenged by race studies; the history of homosexuality , by queer studies; and the history of indigenous people by post colonial studies. 

Lynn Hunts criticism of cultural theories lay in its ability to obscure the meta historical agents in favour of the abstraction of culture. She cites Jean - Francois Lyotard, who writes prophetically the reemergence of meta narratives. Hunt moreover gives greater emphasis on the social history as the medium of meaning making for the human, and the macro processes of history are crucial elements to achieve it.
