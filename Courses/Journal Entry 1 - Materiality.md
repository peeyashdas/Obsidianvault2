For this journal entry on the theme of materiality I wish to discuss the game world and resources in Guild II : Renaissance


A major critique of the world building is the horizontal classification of the occupations instead of a vertical occupation. Drawing from Dyer’s model of social hierarchy, it can be argued that not every occupation in the medieval society occupied the same social strata. Clearly, the merchant was the most important member of the society, followed by craftsmen (master, journeymen and apprentice) , labourers and servants, and finally, the marginals , that constituted the beggars, vagrants and prostitutes. Remarkably, the game world of Guild II , includes playing and non - playing agents from every rank of the society, and goes on to visibilize the marginals as non - playing agents. 

However, as the playing agent , one enters the game one of the following professionals - patron, craftsman, scholar or rogue. These occupations are designed in a linear manner where there is no difference in the access to society between a patron or a guild.
